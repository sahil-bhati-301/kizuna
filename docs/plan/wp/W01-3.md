## Goal
Turn KANJIDIC2 and the Jōyō table into typed kanji and reading rows, with the teach set and official readings marked.

## Acceptance criteria (each must be testable)
1. The parser streams KANJIDIC2 XML into `kanji` rows: literal, codepoint, strokes, classification radical, grade, freq_rank and lang-tagged meanings; nanori are separate `reading` rows of kind `nanori`.
2. Readings pass through the W01-4 normaliser: kana stored in hiragana, okurigana and affix markers split (`た.べる` → stem `た`, okurigana `べる`).
3. Exactly the 2,136 Jōyō kanji get `is_joyo = true`; each reading in the Jōyō table gets `is_joyo = true` on its reading row, and a reading listed only in KANJIDIC2 gets `false`.
4. A Jōyō entry with no matching KANJIDIC2 kanji or reading is reported, not dropped silently.
5. The same inputs give byte-identical output (rows sorted by a stable key; golden file).
6. Every row carries `source`, `source_version` and `license`.

- Q7 answered by the owner (session 9): exclude compatibility ideographs from rows and list them in `report.excludedCharacters`. Original question: KANJIDIC2 has 75 CJK compatibility ideographs (e.g. U+FA67, which NFC folds onto 逸 U+9038). NFC is mandatory, and 74 of them would then duplicate an existing literal (62 are Jōyō), giving duplicate kanji PKs and reading IDs. Provisional behaviour: they are left out of the rows and listed in `report.excludedCharacters` (literal, codepoint, nfcLiteral, reason), so nothing is dropped silently. Alternatives: merge their meanings and readings into the unified kanji, or keep them un-normalised as separate rows.
- Q8 answered by the owner (session 9): follow Q5. `missingKanji` entries become `{kanji, readings[]}`; spec-tester updates joyo.test.ts. Original question: Q5 conflicts with the frozen tests. "Readings attached to its entry" needs `missingKanji` entries to be objects, but joyo.test.ts asserts `missingKanji` toEqual `['丼']` (string[]). Implemented: `missingKanji: string[]` (as the tests require), and those kanji's readings are not repeated in `missingReadings` (as Q5 requires). The readings are not attached anywhere yet; the owner should pick the shape, and spec-tester should then update the test.
- Q9 answered by the owner (session 9): Jōyō matching tries the exact form (markers included) first and ignores markers only when no exact row exists. 生 なま flags only `生:kun:なま`, not `なま-`; 日 か still matches `-か`. Refines Q3.

- Q10 (open, session 10): the Jōyō examples do not settle the okurigana split of two kun readings. Okurigana are derived from the table's example words (rule: an example writes the kanji followed by a suffix of the reading, or by that suffix with its last kana inflected; no such example means no okurigana). Two readings fail it: (1) 干 ひる: all examples are compounds (干上がる, 干物, 潮干狩り), where an ichidan る would drop anyway, so `ひる` and `ひ.る` both fit; KANJIDIC2 has `ひ.る`. (2) 並 なみ: 並の品 writes it with no okurigana, 足並み writes み; KANJIDIC2 has both `なみ` and `な.み`. Both rows are commented out in content/joyo/joyo.tsv (`# PENDING Q10`), so the table has 4,386 of the 4,388 readings until answered. Recommended: 干 `ひ.る`, 並 `なみ` (the stand-alone noun form). Blocks: those two reading flags only.
