## Goal
Fill the three teaching fields on reading rows from the W02-3 alignment, so only official, used readings are drilled.

## Acceptance criteria (each must be testable)
1. `usage_weight` per reading follows Q1, is in 0–1, and is 0 for readings no aligned word uses; `irregular` and `jukujikun` rows count for nothing (digest: excluded from reading-attribution statistics).
2. `is_core` is true exactly when the reading is Jōyō and at least one `is_common` word links to it through `word_kanji`.
3. At most one reading per kanji and kind is `is_primary_of_kind`, chosen by Q2; nanori are never primary.
4. A report lists Jōyō kanji with no core reading of a kind they have Jōyō readings for, and ties broken; the same inputs give byte-identical output (golden on a small fixture: 生, 日, 人, 行).

## Test plan
- Unit: each field rule; irregular and jukujikun excluded.
- Property: at most one primary per (kanji, kind); `is_core` implies `is_joyo`; weights per kanji sum to 1 or all are 0.
- Golden: fixture → reading rows, byte-compared.

## OPEN-Q
- Q1: `usage_weight` is not defined in the spec. Options: (a) the reading's share of its kanji's aligned, non-irregular rows over all loaded words, unweighted (sums to 1 per kanji); (b) as a, but each word weighted by frequency (for example by nf band); (c) as a, common words only. Recommendation: a, because it is simple to explain and the loaded set is already frequency-filtered; b can follow once there is learner data. Answered by the owner (session 12): recommendation taken.
- Q2: "the most frequent" reading of a kind. Options: (a) among core readings of that kind, the highest `usage_weight`, ties by lowest reading ID; none when the kind has no core reading; (b) among all Jōyō readings of that kind, even unused ones. Recommendation: a, so a primary reading always has a common word to teach it with. Answered by the owner (session 12): recommendation taken.
